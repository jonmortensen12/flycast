# Flycast — strategy, roadmap and business notes

Companion to `HANDOFF.md`, which covers the physics. This one covers everything else that
was decided or investigated: platform, performance ceiling, money, legal, and what to build
next in what order.

---

## 1. Platform

**It ships as a WebXR PWA. No port required.** Meta's Horizon Store accepts immersive WebXR
apps packaged with Google's Bubblewrap into an Android App Bundle, and since Connect 2024
those PWAs can take in-app payments through the Digital Goods API. So `index.html` already
is the shippable artifact.

Keep the signing keystore somewhere safe — every future update must use the same certificate.

If a port ever becomes necessary, **Godot over Unity**: MIT licensed with no revenue
threshold at any scale, and Meta has funded its OpenXR support since 2024. Unity Personal is
free below $200K of revenue *generated by the software* (not your salary), and the runtime
fee was scrapped — so Unity is not disqualified, just not preferred.

**Quest 2 has a shelf life.** Feature updates end December 2026, security updates run into
2027. Sideloaded and store apps keep working, but if this becomes a two-year project, target
Quest 3 and stop optimising for 2020 hardware.

## 2. Performance headroom

Measured, not guessed. The current build is **CPU-bound and GPU-idle**:

| | using | Quest 2 budget |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles | ~30k | 350–500k |
| Draw calls | ~20 | 100–200 |
| Physics nodes | ~116 typical, ~265 at finest spacing | — |

The frame cost is dominated by specks, each costing a flow evaluation in JavaScript. Geometry
is nowhere near the limit, which means **there is an order of magnitude of visual headroom
available** before the renderer becomes the constraint.

### What native actually buys

| Path | CPU gain | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| WASM solver (Rust/C++) | 2–3x | moderate |
| Web Worker for physics | ~2x (off the render thread) | moderate |
| Native engine | 3–5x, plus SIMD and real threading | large |

JavaScript is the tax: no SIMD, garbage collection, one thread. **Most of the gap closes
without leaving the browser** — WASM plus a worker recovers the majority of it and keeps the
zero-friction deploy loop.

One gotcha: sharing memory with a worker needs `SharedArrayBuffer`, which needs COOP/COEP
headers, which **GitHub Pages cannot set**. Cloudflare Pages or Netlify can, and are
otherwise equivalent.

Where native genuinely wins is **assets, not arithmetic**: compressed textures, streaming,
baked lighting, better culling, lower draw-call overhead. If the ambition is richly textured
environments with vegetation, that is the argument for porting — not solver speed.

### The biggest single optimisation

The flow field is analytic, so specks can be advected entirely in a shader. **Implemented**
as of the GPU speck path: each speck re-integrates its own streamline in the vertex shader,
20,000 of them, for zero CPU cost — roughly 0.5% of the GPU. Toggle in the menu, CPU path
retained for comparison.

## 3. Feature cost analysis

**Nearly free** — logic and content only, no meaningful compute:

- **Fly selection and hatch.** A fly is already just data: mass, diameter, drag, buoyancy.
  A hatch is a time-driven table of what is on the water and what fish are keyed to. Highest
  gameplay value per unit of work on the whole list.
- **Nymphing, streamers, sinking line, droppers.** Parameter changes plus a second fly node.
  Sinking line is density above water with no film clamp — the existing model handles it.
- **Species and environments.** Mesh and parameter variants.
- **Game modes.** Pure logic.
- **Wind.** A vector added to the air-drag relative velocity. Transforms casting difficulty
  for almost no code.

**Moderate:**

- **Snagging.** Trees and brush as capsule colliders against line nodes — a few thousand
  tests per frame. The interesting design work is the *unsnagging*.
- **Richer fish behaviour.** A handful of agents, so compute is irrelevant. The cost is
  design: feeding rhythm, refusals, memory of being pricked, spooking spreading between fish.

**Expensive, and one is a genuine unlock:**

- **Real rivers.** Terrain is available from USGS 3DEP lidar; *bathymetry* — the underwater
  shape that determines everything — almost never is. This is a data-acquisition problem, not
  a compute one.
- **Dynamic shallow-water grid solve** replacing the analytic field. A 256x64 grid at 30 Hz
  in WASM is feasible. This is what would let arbitrary river geometry *produce* its own
  currents instead of hand-authored fields, and it is the prerequisite for real rivers being
  worth doing. Note it composes well with GPU specks: the shader would sample a flow texture
  instead of computing analytically, which is cheaper, not harder.

### Not on the original list but ranked higher than parts of it

- **The haul.** Single and double hauls are the main casting skill still missing, and the
  line model can already express them.
- **Roll cast and spey.** Necessary the moment there are trees behind you.
- **A strike window.** Takes are currently automatic. Making the player set the hook, and
  lose fish to slack line, adds more skill per line of code than anything else available.
- **Audio.** Badly underrated. River sound spatialised by *local* flow speed, the reel's
  click as line leaves the spool, line whistle on a fast stroke, the plop of a rise. Probably
  the largest immersion gain per hour of work in the whole project.
- **Losing fish.** Hook pulls on slack, tippet fails at a nick. Failure makes success mean
  something.

## 4. Suggested sequence

1. Finish dialling the cast. Nothing else matters until that is right.
2. GPU specks. *(done — verify it reads as well as the CPU version before deleting anything.)*
3. Wind, strike window, audio. Cheapest large gains available.
4. Flies and hatch. The first feature that turns a simulator into a game.
5. Only then decide on WASM or native, informed by what is actually slow.

## 5. Money

Honest assessment, with the caveat that this is a market analysis and not a forecast.

**The ceiling exists and someone reached it.** Real VR Fishing has made about $15M since 2019
across roughly a million users, at $19.99, from a funded studio with six years of DLC. They
are now adding a fly fishing mode, which says the niche is real and that they would
eventually be a competitor.

**The market is harder than that suggests.** Just over 100 titles grossed more than $1M in
2025, with store revenue up only slightly year over year — and that is gross, before Meta's
30%. Meta's own data shows new customers spending less than older ones, older ones also
spending less, and the incoming mix shifting toward 13–24 year olds. Developers held a GDC
panel about a "VR visibility crisis."

**Distribution of outcomes for a solo first release:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Most likely | $0–3,000 |
| Good | $10,000–40,000 — needs polish, a trailer that travels, fly-fishing YouTube pickup |
| Great | $100,000+ — needs the casting to become *the* thing fly fishers talk about |

Against perhaps 800–1,500 hours. As an hourly rate that is poor, and against a senior
industry salary it is clearly negative on a pure dollar basis. Build it because the problem
is interesting; treat revenue as a surprise.

**The genuine edge:** almost nobody builds real casting physics, because it is hard and most
players do not demand it. The competition sells scenery and calm. A domain expert with an
ergonomics background building the thing a fly fisher actually wants is a defensible moat.

**The angle worth pursuing if money mattered:** do not sell it as a game. Fly casting is
*taught*, expensively, and cannot be practised indoors in winter. A casting trainer with real
rod loading, drag-free drift scoring and instrumented feedback is a different product with a
different buyer — fly shops, guide services, casting instructors, FFI certification
candidates. Smaller audience, far higher willingness to pay, no competition from a funded
studio's DLC schedule, and it plays directly to the metrics the rig already computes.

## 6. Legal and structure

Not legal or tax advice; worth twenty minutes with a professional once real money is plausible.

**An LLC is not required.** Meta will pay an individual as a sole proprietor. An LLC buys
liability separation and a cleaner business identity. In Washington it is cheap — a couple
hundred dollars to form, a small annual report, plus B&O tax on gross receipts. Forming it
*before* shipping is a common way to spend money on a project that never ships. Form it when
publishing becomes real.

**The thing that actually matters first:** employer invention-assignment and outside-work
policy. Washington's RCW 49.44.140 limits what an employer can claim — broadly, inventions
made on your own time, with your own equipment, unrelated to the employer's business — which
likely protects a fly fishing game. "Likely" is not "checked." Read the employment agreement
and the outside-work policy before publishing anything under your own name, and use any
approval process that exists. This is a bigger risk to the project than corporate structure
and costs nothing to resolve now.

**Order of operations:** confirm you are clear at work → ship free as a hosted URL and see if
anyone cares → LLC and store listing only if the answer is yes.
